Investigation · MIDA / SB 316

The Senator Who Wrote His Own Board's Rulebook

Sen. Jerry Stevenson is Vice Chair of the Military Installation Development Authority. In 2025 he personally sponsored the bill that expanded MIDA's own powers — including letting MIDA review the environmental impact of its own projects. Thirteen months later, his board used that power to approve Stratos. The same year, on a second state development authority, he personally moved financing for a different developer's project — and had to vote on it twice after his own committee initially said no.

I.The Vice Chair

A senator sits on the board he's about to legislate for

Sen. Jerry W. Stevenson (SD-6) is MIDA's sitting Vice Chair — confirmed on the record in the board's own June 24, 2025 minutes, alongside Chair Stuart Adams, Mark Shepherd, Gary Harter, Mike Ostermiller, and Gage Froerer.

Stevenson isn't a passive appointee. Until his retirement from the Senate, he chaired Executive Appropriations — the Senate's top budget committee, a seat now passing to Tami Tran (see Movement V). He also sits on the Point of the Mountain State Land Authority board, a separate state development authority, where a prior investigation found him voting on his own "cleanup bill" for that same authority — a pattern of sponsoring legislation that touches boards he personally sits on.

Worth noting — none of this is illegal. Utah law allows officials to serve on multiple boards and sponsor legislation affecting them. What follows is the documented record of what that overlap produced.
Not a new pattern — same seat, different agency
Stevenson is also a sitting UIPA board member, and he personally motioned the PCC LAND LLC development agreement at UIPA's May 20, 2024 meeting — the agreement at the center of this newsroom's Little Mountain Service Area investigation.
UIPA Board Meeting Minutes, May 20, 2024. Full story: The Little Mountain Service Area investigation →

UIPA and MIDA run on the same basic mechanism — tax differential, board-adopted project areas, minimal outside review. Stevenson sits on both boards. What follows is what happens when he also writes the law for one of them.

II.The Bill

SB 316: Stevenson's board gets to grade its own homework

Full title: "Military Installation Development Authority and Other Development Zone Amendments." Chief Sponsor: Sen. Jerry W. Stevenson. House Sponsor: Rep. Val L. Peterson.

The bill did three things to MIDA's taxing power, and one thing to its regulatory power:

What SB 316 (2025) actually changed
50%
New "eligible basic special district" category gets the same 50% sales-tax cut MIDA already claims — effective Jan 1, 2026
100%
MIDA can capture up to 100% of its own resort-communities tax revenue in "qualified development zones" — stacked on top of the base 50% cut
3rd Sub
The House's third substitute added a power not in any earlier version: MIDA may act as "lead agency" for environmental review of its own project areas

That last change is the one that matters most for what happened thirteen months later. Before SB 316, MIDA's projects were subject to environmental review by an outside authority. After it, MIDA can review itself.

Who presented the bill
MIDA's own staff personally lobbied for the bill at both chambers of the Legislature.
Ariana Farber, MIDA Deputy Director — presented in the Senate committee.
Nicole Cottle, MIDA Chief Legal & Administrative Officer — presented in the House committee.

MIDA didn't just benefit from SB 316. Its own leadership showed up and argued for it — while its Vice Chair carried it as the bill's sponsor.

IV.Thirteen Months

The power gets used — on the exact project his board approves

SB 316 was signed March 27, 2025. On April 24, 2026 — roughly thirteen months later — MIDA's board used the new "lead agency" environmental-review authority to approve the Stratos Project Area Plan. Stevenson voted yes.

  • MARCH 27, 2025SB 316 signed into law. MIDA can now act as its own environmental lead agency.
  • APRIL 24, 2026 — 13 MONTHS LATERMIDA's board votes on four resolutions tied to Stratos: adopting the Project Area Plan, development standards, the O'Leary development agreement, and the Box Elder County interlocal agreement.
  • SAME MEETINGStevenson votes yes on all four.
"The bill Stevenson personally sponsored, with MIDA's own staff lobbying for it, was directly used roughly 13 months later to approve the exact project his own board then voted on." — documented sequence, MIDA board minutes, April 24, 2026

This is the through-line of the whole bill: a sitting board member sponsors the law that lets his own agency skip outside environmental review, his agency's staff personally lobby the Legislature for it, and just over a year later that same board — with him voting — uses the power on a live project.

Sources
MIDA Board Meeting Minutes, April 24, 2026 (Stratos Project Area Plan, development standards, O'Leary development agreement, Box Elder County interlocal agreement — four resolutions, Stevenson voting yes on each).
V.The Money

Funding his own successor

Stevenson personally donated to Tami Tran — the Kaysville mayor and SD-6 nominee inheriting both his Senate seat and, per the established pattern, his Executive Appropriations chairmanship. He didn't donate alone, and he didn't donate once.

Fellow MIDA board member Stuart Adams — MIDA's Chair — also donated to Tran's campaign in the same window. Two members of the same board that approved Stratos are funding the succession of the seat that oversees the state's budget, including the appropriations that touch MIDA's own projects.

VI.The Other Board

Same senator, a second authority, the same playbook

MIDA isn't the only development authority Stevenson sits on. He's also a voting member of the Utah Inland Port Authority (UIPA) board — the agency behind Weber County's West Weber project area, covered in this newsroom's Little Mountain Service Area investigation. In 2025, the pattern repeated: Stevenson personally moved financing for a private developer, then had to vote on it again after his own committee initially rejected it.

  • JUNE 23, 2025UIPA's Authority Infrastructure Bank Loan Committee recommends a $5 million, 3-year loan to PCC Land, LLC — the Gardner Company/Black Pine entity behind West Weber's Promontory Commerce Center — for sewer infrastructure.
  • JUNE 26, 2025At the full UIPA board meeting, Board Member Stevenson personally makes the motion to approve the $5 million loan. It passes unanimously.
  • SEPTEMBER 15, 2025The loan reaches the Legislature's Executive Appropriations Committee — which Stevenson chairs — for final execution approval. It fails, 9-8-3. Stevenson votes yes; the loan fails anyway.
  • SAME MEETINGSen. Kirk Cullimore, one of the eight "no" votes, moves to reconsider. On reconsideration, the loan passes.
    • UIPA Board Minutes, June 26, 2025
    "Board Member Stevenson moved that the Utah Inland Port Authority Board approves an infrastructure loan from the Inland Port Authority Revolving Loan Fund of $5 million to PCC Land, LLC."
    Board Chair Osborne seconded. Passed unanimously. Full story: The Little Mountain Service Area investigation →

    The same three principals from the West Weber land assembly — Daniel Stephens, Brad Margetts, and Dallin Curriden — were personally in the room at both the loan committee's approval and the full board vote. This is the identical structure documented at MIDA: a board member with a network of overlapping seats personally advances financing that benefits people who show up, in person, at every stage of the approval.
