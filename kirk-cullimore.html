I.Majority Leader

Six confirmation committees, and a family-first campaign

Sen. Kirk A. Cullimore Jr. (SD-19) has served since 2019 and became Senate Majority Leader on January 21, 2025. He's an attorney, a former music teacher and small-business owner, and — per his own campaign site — the father of six raising his family in Sandy with his wife, Heather. His public framing is "Family Comes First"; his committee footprint is one of the broadest in the Senate.

His committee seats
6
Senate confirmation committees — Business and Labor, Judicial, Judiciary, Law Enforcement and Criminal Justice, Retirement and Independent Entities, Revenue and Taxation
22
Total committee seats, including Executive Appropriations and the Economic and Community Development Appropriations Subcommittee

Confirmation committees vet the governor's appointees to state boards and agencies before the full Senate votes. Sitting on six of them, plus the budget subcommittee that funds the Governor's Office of Economic Opportunity, gives him a hand at several stages of state governance at once — writing law, vetting appointees, and controlling the money. None of that is unusual for Senate leadership on its own. What follows is where those seats intersect with his own outside interests.

II.The Insurance Company

Effective immediately, disclosed 91 days later

On April 2, 2026, a Miami-based insurance holding company announced that Utah's Senate Majority Leader was now the president of one of its subsidiaries. State law gave him a week to tell the Senate. He told it three months later.

A-CAP / Business Wire, April 2, 2026, 6:13 PM ET
"With a strong foundation in Utah and the confidence of our regulatory partners, we are well positioned to grow responsibly, support policyholders, and deliver on our long-term commitments."
Kirk Cullimore, quoted in the release naming him President of Sentinel Security Life Insurance Company, "effective immediately." The release notes he'd already been "a member of Sentinel's Board of Directors since 2025," and describes his day job: "he also serves in the Utah State Senate... including his current position as Senate Majority Leader."
DateWhat the record shows

His relationship to Sentinel wasn't new to his paperwork — it changed category. Two filings dated January 2, 2026 already listed Sentinel, but only as a board seat. The presidency itself first appears on July 2, moved up to the section for current employment and ownership, described there as "President of Life Insurance Company." Sentinel Security Life is Utah-domiciled (NAIC 68802) and regulated by the Utah Insurance Department. Cullimore sits on the Senate Business and Labor Committee, which has jurisdiction over insurance legislation, and on its Confirmation Committee, which reviews the governor's appointees — including the state insurance commissioner — before the full Senate votes to confirm them. He's also a disclosed board member of Haymarket Insurance Company, a reinsurance firm.

Worth noting — this arrangement is structural, not a documented instance of a recorded vote. The current insurance commissioner was confirmed years before Cullimore joined Sentinel's board. What's established is the standing setup: the Senate Majority Leader now leads a company regulated by an agency whose next leader, whenever a vacancy occurs, would be vetted by a committee he sits on.

III.The Institute

He sponsored the law. The board it created doesn't have his seat written into it.

In 2025, Cullimore was the Senate Sponsor of HB 530, which reorganized the Utah Innovation Lab into the Nucleus Institute. The law's seven-member board has no legislator seat — it's the executive director, the director of Talent Ready Utah, and five people "with relevant experience" jointly appointed by the commissioner, the executive director, and the Talent Ready Utah director. If Cullimore sits on that board, it's as one of those five discretionary picks — a separate act from sponsoring the bill that created it.

  • 2025 SESSIONCullimore is the Senate Sponsor of HB 530, creating the Nucleus Institute.
  • MAY 7, 2025The Act takes effect.
  • AUG 27, 2025Public launch coverage — Cullimore participates as the bill's Senate sponsor.
  • NOV 10, 2025Nucleus's own website sitemap dates the last modification of Cullimore's individual "Board Member" page to this day.
  • JAN 2, 2026Cullimore's conflict of interest filings do not list Nucleus — consistent with the corporation not yet being formally incorporated at that point.
  • JAN 30, 2026Nucleus Institute's Articles of Incorporation are filed. Seven director seats are provided for; none are named in the filing.
  • JULY 2, 2026Cullimore amends his disclosure to add the Sentinel presidency. The Nucleus Institute is not listed.
  • CURRENTNucleus's "Our Team" page identifies Cullimore as a board member.
    • Written into the bill Cullimore himself carried in the Senate
    "The institute and the Utah innovation fund are not subject to: (a) Title 52, Chapter 4, Open and Public Meetings Act; or (b) Title 63G, Chapter 2, Government Records Access and Management Act." — Enrolled H.B. 530 (2025), Utah Code 53B-37-102(3), Senate Sponsor: Kirk A. Cullimore

    Most state boards, commissions, and quasi-public entities in Utah are subject to both laws — the public can see when a board met, who showed up, how a vote went, and can request records if something doesn't add up. HB 530 writes the Nucleus Institute and its $40 million Utah Innovation Fund out of both requirements, in the same bill that created the board Cullimore appears to sit on. Two concrete consequences follow directly from that exemption:

  • NO MEETING RECORDThere is no public minutes requirement for whatever meeting or vote added Cullimore to the board — so there's no document that could independently confirm when it happened, who voted, or whether he recused himself from any part of it.
  • NO RECORDS REQUESTA GRAMA request — the normal tool for compelling a public body to produce records it hasn't voluntarily posted — doesn't apply here. That's not a gap in this investigation's effort; it's the specific legal effect of the statute Cullimore sponsored.
    • What the record does and doesn't establish
    The Nucleus Institute currently identifies Sen. Kirk Cullimore as a board member. Its official sitemap dates the last modification of his individual board-member page to November 10, 2025 — almost eight months before his July 2, 2026 filing. The filing does not list the Nucleus Institute. No appointment letter or board record establishing his precise start date has been located, so the website date should not be described as the formal appointment date.
    This is the practical result of the exemption above: ordinarily, a GRAMA request or a public meeting minute would settle this in an afternoon. Here, neither tool is available.

    This is an apparently unlisted affiliation and a documented open question, not a settled finding of a failed disclosure — the precise date he joined the board isn't publicly available, and without it, no seven-day clock can be timed. What is established: he sits on the Economic and Community Development Appropriations Subcommittee, which funds the Governor's Office of Economic Opportunity — the office run by his apparent fellow Nucleus board member, Jefferson Moss. The institute also manages a state-affiliated venture fund whose individual investment decisions, under this same exemption, are made without the public visibility that would apply to almost any comparable state body.

    V.The Bills

    Gatekeeper for AI and data, sponsor of tort reform, floor sponsor for healthcare

    Thirteen AI, data, and digital-identity bills moved through the 2026 session with Cullimore as either sponsor or floor sponsor — more than any other senator. Three of his bills sit directly next to donor patterns worth naming plainly.

    SB 211 — Tort Amendments (his own bill, still pending)
    Robert J Debry & Associates: $15,000+. Utah Association for Justice / Lawyers Involved for Utah: recurring, including a single $10,000 check. Craig Swapp & Associates, Siegfried & Jensen, Driggs Bills & Day: recurring smaller amounts, nearly every reporting period.
    He is chief sponsor of tort legislation while that donor base funds him. The bill and the donor pattern are both matters of public record; no motive is asserted here.
    HB 071 — Health Provider Directory and Access Amendments (floor sponsor, signed)
    Cullimore is a disclosed board member of Common Spirit Utah's Board of Community Advisors. His donor base separately includes SelectHealth, Regence, MountainStar Healthcare, Molina, UnitedHealth, and Cigna.
    The same structural pattern as the Sentinel finding: a healthcare-sector board seat, healthcare-sector legislation, and healthcare-sector donations, together.

    Cullimore also floor-sponsored HB 447, Tobacco Amendments — his donor record separately includes Altria Client Services LLC and RAI Services Company. On the AI and data cluster: he sponsored SB 275 (State-Endorsed Digital Identity Program Amendments, signed) and floor-sponsored HB 320 (Office of AI Policy Amendments, which amends the office his own 2024 bill created), HB 450 (Data Privacy Amendments), and HB 220 (Public Safety Data Amendments) — thirteen bills total in that cluster in one session.

    One of his own bills didn't pass: SB 337 (2025) would have created the Beehive Development Agency — a powerful new state authority the Salt Lake Tribune called "the one authority to rule them all," designed to coordinate with MIDA, the Inland Port Authority, and several other land-use bodies. It died without a Senate floor vote after cross-partisan opposition. A fuller look at that bill, and how it connects to Utah's broader pattern of land-use authorities, follows separately.

    VI.The Entities

    A law practice, a second LLC, and his wife's name on both

    Cullimore's disclosed law-practice employer is KCJR, LLC. A second entity, Fuerza Legal, LLC, sits underneath it — and his wife Heather is a named member of the parent entity.

    KCJR, LLC — formed April 20, 2021, "legal and consulting services." The original Certificate of Organization lists two members: Kirk Cullimore and his wife, Heather Cullimore, both at the same Draper address. It's KCJR that appears on his conflict of interest filings as his employer.

    Fuerza Legal, LLC — formed July 3, 2024. KCJR, LLC is both a member and its registered agent; Cullimore is also a direct member. No public-facing practice presence exists under this name. It doesn't appear by name on his disclosure filings, though it's unconfirmed whether it's meant to be captured under the KCJR listing itself. This is presented as documented entity structure, not a claimed disclosure gap.
